# Goal

Help learners ask whether rest is restoring them for love and responsibility, or whether comfort and friction avoidance have become governing desires.

# Discernment dimensions

Use scenarios and questions around:

- postponing a difficult but rightful responsibility;
- leaving every uncomfortable conversation;
- choosing entertainment over promised care;
- avoiding hidden service without recognition;
- using “self-care” language to evade repentance;
- using “self-denial” language to justify overwork, danger or abuse.

# Workflow

1. Implement curriculum scenarios with clear authority labels.
2. Distinguish four states: healthy rest, legitimate boundary, recovery/support need, avoidant comfort rule.
3. Do not infer from one missed task.
4. Let the learner choose one small responsibility or service practice.
5. Provide minimum actions and safe exits.
6. Connect relational harm to repair.
7. Connect exhaustion, illness, abuse or crisis to appropriate support.
8. Integrate selected practices into Formation Plan without raising plan capacity.

# Guardrails

Never praise:

- chronic sleep loss;
- unsafe work;
- remaining with an abuser;
- ignoring disability or illness;
- refusing professional care;
- neglecting dependents in order to complete a “spiritual challenge.”

# Tests

Cover healthy-rest classification, boundary classification, avoidant pattern, unsafe relationship, burnout risk, service selection, minimum action, plan max-three enforcement, non-shaming copy and safety handoff.

# Definition of done

The module calls adults toward costly love and responsibility while honoring creaturely limits, safety, recovery and grace.
